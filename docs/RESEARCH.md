# Research notes

Compiled from web-search summaries and abstracts, **not full-paper reads**. Treat each line as a lead to verify. Confidence tags describe how well-established a claim looks. The richer, formatted version is `prototype/time-intel-brief.html`.

## How the brain senses time
- Interval timing (milliseconds to seconds) runs through basal ganglia, cerebellum and cortical circuits; the striatal beat frequency model is prominent. *Strong.* [Annual Reviews](https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev-neuro-062012-170349), [Neuropsychopharmacology](https://www.nature.com/articles/npp2010113)
- Scalar property: timing error grows in proportion to duration (Weber-like). Holds in children at all ages tested; sensitivity improves into adolescence. *Strong.* [Droit-Volet 2002](https://doi.org/10.1080/02724980244000161), [Current Psychology 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12144-022-03526-9)
- Emotion and attention distort duration; secondary tasks shorten perceived time more in children, in proportion to attentional capacity. *Strong.* [BPS overview](https://www.bps.org.uk/psychologist/children-and-time), [Anger and time perception](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/6453709_Anger_and_time_perception_in_children)
- Circadian / time-of-day perception in children: **not yet searched.**

## Development, roughly by age
- Infants: a primitive sense of time. Toddlers: time tied to routine events. *Strong.* [Temporal concepts](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6290033/), [Events and children's sense of time](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4356002/)
- Before about 4, learned durations do not transfer to new contexts. *Mixed.* [Neurodevelopmental approach](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22999968/)
- 3–4: well-organised scripts for familiar routines; some episodic foresight at 3, a spurt around 3½–4. *Moderate.* [Scripts](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022096517304046), [Foresight 3–5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19781693/), [Memory and foresight](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0885201411000578)
- About 4: "tomorrow" understood; about 4–5: can order the main events of a day; under 5, conventional time has little influence. *Moderate.* [Friedman 1976](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED136960.pdf)
- 3-year-olds struggle to wait for future rewards; delays over about 5 minutes are hard. *Moderate.* [Delay of gratification](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8415579/)
- Left-to-right mental timeline appears about 5–6, follows writing/calendar direction, and is culturally acquired. *Moderate.* [Emergence](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4489723/), [Duration timeline](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.01155/full), [Tillman 2022](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/cdev.13780)
- Analogue clock reading is a multi-year skill (kindergarten to grade 2); exposure and teaching matter more than maturity. Typical errors: minute hand read as hours; drifting hour hand. Digital is generally learned sooner. *Strong.* [Analogue vs digital](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF03217308), [Clock reading](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1258500.pdf)
- No evidence found that the round clock face is inherited; it appears to be cultural familiarity.

## Pictures and screens
- Photographs are recognised more reliably than line drawings; 3-year-olds were barely above chance identifying what a drawing stood for. *Solid.* [Line-drawing recognition](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-44529-9)
- Toddlers under about 2.5 often fail to use pictures as information; dual representation develops in the third year. *Solid.* [Toddlers' referential understanding](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2865246/)
- Video deficit: learning from a screen is weaker than from a live person, though caregiver co-viewing closes much of the gap at 2–4. *Solid.* [Encyclopedia summary](https://www.child-encyclopedia.com/technology-early-childhood-education/according-experts/infants-toddlers-and-learning-screen-media), [Co-viewing at 2](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.576940/full)
- Video modelling: moderate effect for preschoolers, mostly in special-education studies. *Moderate.* [Video models for transitions](https://doi.org/10.1177/10538151251353525)

## Visual schedules, timers, colour
- A visual timer study (44 children) found lower anticipatory anxiety and fewer inattentive behaviours, most for children at higher ADHD risk; only some children watched it. Much wider support is from practitioner sites. *Mixed.* [Time on Their Side](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12731990/)
- Visual schedules and transition supports: mostly autism and practitioner literature. *Weak for typical toddlers.* [Visual schedules and transitions](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/bin.70028)
- Red/green stop/go is learnable by 3 per early-learning guides (weak). Red did not reliably lengthen perceived time; blue was overestimated more in one study; effect of red varied by sex in another. *Mixed.* [Blue overestimated](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-018-19892-z), [Red and sex](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep05899)
- Red-green colour deficiency: about 4% of boys globally, 2.6–7.6% in US boys. *Solid.* [Preschool prevalence](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4839481/)

## Gaps
Circadian and time-of-day; cross-cultural non-clock time systems; experimental evidence for non-clock displays with typical 2–4-year-olds; full-paper reads; design precedents; screen-time effects of a carried device.
